BY THE LOCAL NEWS DESK   |   BELLWEATHER ROW

A thief with remarkably poor business sense has been at work on Bellweather Row. Among the missing items: forty-six doorbells, a crooked cafe sign, and the bottom step of a staircase.

Cash tills remain untouched. At one address, the intruder even tightened a loose hinge.

“If it was a burglar, they were tidier than my last employee,” said Ada, owner of the corner cafe at No. 18.

Police have described the case as “technically a priority.”

BY THE REPORTER WHO STAYED FOR THE LAST BUS

Last night, a street gathered inside a warehouse. Its sign hung at the wrong angle, its lamps faced the right way, and every doorbell rang a tune somebody knew.

Nell, Bellweather Row's retired caretaker, had borrowed its smallest landmarks to build a place for one last evening together. What began as an absurd crime report became a collection of names.

Ada brought coffee and an ordinary Tuesday on a cassette. Mina brought a light that had stayed on for children with torn uniforms. June and Ivo carried twenty years of pencil marks without once arguing about who should take which end.

They gave Nell a doorplate with her own name on it. For forty years she had known where everyone belonged. They wanted her to know that she belonged somewhere too.

Then the buses came. People took their things, their plans, and their imperfect promises to stay in touch. The little street came apart because its pieces still had lives to go to.

The old buildings will still come down on Monday. There was no last-minute rescue for them. This was not that kind of story.

But nobody who left the warehouse was merely an address being cleared from a map.

THE END
THREE WEEKS LATER

The first reunion is rained off. They try again.

At Ada's cart, somebody straightens the old sign. Three people immediately object. Mina unpacks a visitor's chair. June arrives carrying Ivo's umbrella.

Nell comes last, because for once she did not have to open up.

The little bell rings two notes. The second is still wrong.
